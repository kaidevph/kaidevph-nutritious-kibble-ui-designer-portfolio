UI dissection

Each screen is treated as a composition: visual hierarchy first, then component anatomy, spacing, imagery, and conversion intent.

01 · Hero / Landing

Emotion leads. Action follows.

The visual-first entry screen establishes the brand, emotional tone, and primary action.

A

Brand anchor

The small white brand lockup sits at the top with strong contrast against the purple field, creating immediate ownership of the screen.

B

Headline hierarchy

Large stacked type and a heavy shadow treatment make the message the strongest text object in the composition.

C

Subject-first imagery

The pet occupies most of the viewport and becomes the emotional focal point before product information is processed.

D

CTA contrast

The lime/yellow button breaks the purple field and creates a clear single-action exit point: “Shop Now”.

✓
Design read: deliberately minimal. One message, one image story, one CTA. The interface relies on contrast and scale rather than dense navigation.
02 · Home / Category Hub

Turn the small viewport into a storefront.

A compact storefront balances promotion, discovery, and category browsing inside a narrow mobile viewport.

A

Compact global header

Menu, brand, and cart are compressed into one purple bar so utility stays visible without dominating the page.

B

Promotion strip

The discount banner creates urgency and gives the page a second visual beat before the search field.

C

Modular discovery grid

Large promotional tiles and smaller categories create a clear rhythm for quick scanning and tap targets.

D

Informational labels

Short labels under imagery help users decode category tiles without requiring a second interaction.

+
Design read: information-dense, but the tile structure keeps the density organized. The main opportunity is to protect readability as more merchandising modules are added.
03 · Product Detail

Conversion happens through structured proof.

The product page combines imagery, price, social proof, product copy, variants, and the purchase action into one decision-focused screen.

A

Contextual back bar

The purple header preserves brand continuity while the back control gives users an obvious return to browsing.

B

Product proof cluster

Price, rating, review count, and product metadata sit close to the title, helping users evaluate value quickly.

C

Variant shelf

Alternative flavors are presented as visual cards, reducing cognitive load compared with a text-only selector.

D

Purchase emphasis

Quantity controls and the large rounded add-to-cart action form the final conversion block near the decision point.

↗
Design read: the page moves from reassurance to choice to action. It is the most information-rich screen, so grouping and spacing do the heavy lifting.

Designer’s audit

What the visual design does well — and where a production-ready pass could push it further.

✓ Strong visual identity

Purple + lime creates a memorable retail personality and gives the CTA a natural home.

✓ Clear emotional positioning

Pet photography is not decoration; it functions as the primary brand asset and reinforces warmth.

✓ Good hierarchy at a glance

Each screen has a dominant focal point that explains the page purpose quickly.

△ Readability under density

On the home screen, tile labels and very small utility text could benefit from stronger size and contrast rules.

△ Consistency opportunity

Button shapes, metadata, badges, and icon treatments can be normalized into a reusable component spec.

△ Accessibility pass

A production version should validate contrast, type size, hit-area dimensions, and image alternatives across states.
